# WeedIntel™ Platform Analysis

**Platform reviewed:** [WeedIntel™ — Herbicide GTM Intelligence Platform](https://gvl-weedintel.web.app/#/segmentation/opportunity)  
**Reviewed:** 5 November 2026  
**Default dashboard context observed:** Kharif 2024 · Maharashtra · Maize · Ashitaka

## 1. What WeedIntel is

WeedIntel is presented as a go-to-market (GTM) intelligence and planning platform for herbicides. It brings field and market signals together to help a company answer:

1. **Where is weed pressure high?**
2. **Where is there a market or product opportunity?**
3. **Which competitor or product currently controls the market?**
4. **Which districts and mandals should the field team prioritize?**
5. **What should the team do, when should it do it, and how should execution be measured?**
6. **Is there a potential unmet need that merits a new product or launch?**

In plain language, it is intended to connect **market understanding → geography prioritization → recommended field actions → execution tracking → management reporting**.

The site labels itself as a live platform, but the views inspected show a historical Kharif 2024 data context and some 2025 planning references. The visible data should therefore be treated as an example or planning scenario until its source, refresh date, and definitions are confirmed.

## 2. How to read the platform

### Global context and navigation

The left-side filters shown on the segmentation screen include **season, state, crop, and product**. The inspected selection is Kharif 2024, Maharashtra, Maize, and Ashitaka. The available choices vary by filter and page; not every view exposes the same controls.

The main workspaces are:

- **Strategy Command Center** — portfolio-level strategy and priority plays.
- **Market Intelligence** — survey, weed, competitor, district, and execution views.
- **Segmentation Tool** — opportunity tiers, weed hotspots, competitive conditions, and mandal-level detail.
- **Product Launch Roadmap** — unmet need, concept selection, launch prioritization, and readiness.
- **Reports** — report configuration, preview, export, and sharing.

### Common terms

- **GTM:** Go-to-market — how a product is positioned, distributed, promoted, and sold.
- **Mandal:** A sub-district administrative area used for local targeting.
- **DAS:** Days after sowing. The platform repeatedly highlights a 12–20 DAS crop/control window. Treat timing and product instructions as items to validate against approved local agronomy and product labels.
- **TAM / SAM / SOM:** Common market-sizing terms: total addressable market, serviceable available market, and serviceable obtainable market. The platform also uses **TPM** (“Total Potential Market”) in one view; its exact definition is not provided.
- **Infestation / opportunity / vulnerability scores:** Numeric prioritization signals. The interface displays scores, but does not explain the underlying formulas, calibration, or confidence intervals.
- **₹, Cr, L, kL, ac:** Indian rupees, crore, lakh, kilolitre, and acres.

## 3. Workspaces and what each one shows

### A. Strategy Command Center

This is the high-level portfolio and strategy landing page. It organizes strategy into six pillars:

1. Prioritize hotspot districts.
2. Act inside the spray window.
3. Scale direct-attack mandals.
4. Lead with pack and economics.
5. Fast-track a selective product concept.
6. Prepare a launch wave.

The first four have strategy-entry actions; the concept-validation and launch-readiness pillars are marked **Coming Soon** in the inspected view.

The rest of the page provides:

- **“See the Market” map and sizing:** A state map, coverage figures, market-size labels (TPM/TAM/SAM/SOM), competitor shares, and strategic observations.
- **Mandal Opportunity Heat Map:** A geographic view of opportunity, paired with a playbook framework: Direct-Attack, Tank-Mix, Premix, and Monitor.
- **Direct-Attack Opportunity & Potential:** A shortlist of mandals with acreage, potential value, and a score/percentage.
- **Pack + Economics Preview:** A recommended pack and modeled uplift/ROI/savings.
- **“Build the Future Portfolio”:** A whitespace view, launch opportunity/readiness scores, recommended concept, and proposed launch waves.

**How to use it:** Start here for the strategic storyline and candidate priorities; use the other workspaces to inspect the evidence and local detail behind those choices.

### B. Market Intelligence

This workspace has three views.

#### 1. Executive Summary

The summary cards report surveyed villages and acreage, weed infestation, targetable acreage, expected herbicide demand, strategy coverage, execution score, incremental sales, and liquidation. The page also includes:

- **Maharashtra Weed Infestation Map:** A district-level map colored by infestation index. The displayed legend is Severe (80–100), High (60–80), Medium (40–60), and Low (0–40). Selecting a district populates local survey and infestation details.
- **Top Weeds table and acreage chart:** Ranks weed species by infestation percentage, number of districts, and acreage.
- **Selected District Spotlight:** Local infestation, surveyed footprint, leading weeds, opportunity, and crop/control window.
- **Executive Insights:** Narrative observations and proposed priorities.
- **Competitor Market Share:** Brand share and a target share reference.
- **Performance Summary:** Revenue, secondary sales, liquidation, farmers reached, and a trend chart.

The map is a **pressure map**, not by itself a sales-opportunity map: high infestation can indicate need, but commercial attractiveness also depends on product fit, channel access, competition, timing, and other factors.

#### 2. District Intelligence

A district selector drives a district-focused profile. The inspected Nanded view includes:

- A Maharashtra district map and priority-district list.
- District risk, infestation index, surveyed villages/acreage, opportunity score, and top weeds.
- Weed composition and a multi-season infestation-index trend.
- A **Major Weed Control Window** timeline, recommended application timing, and product references.
- Local competitor share, activity, trend, and threat.
- Company presence, village/outlet coverage, and a coverage-versus-share gap.
- Commercial results such as revenue, share, liquidation, acreage, farmer reach, and ROI.
- A **Next Best Actions** list with priority labels.

This view is for moving from a state-level signal to a district-level diagnosis and action checklist.

#### 3. Strategy & Execution

This view connects opportunity scoring to field work:

- **Herbicide Opportunity Engine:** A district scorecard with columns for weed infestation, product fit, crop stage, field strength, channel strength, competitor weakness, opportunity score, and priority. The interface explicitly shows weights for some components (25%, 25%, 15%, and 10%); it does not make all component weights equally clear.
- **Geography Prioritization Matrix:** Districts plotted into four action quadrants—Build Access, Attack Now, Monitor, and Defend—using infestation/opportunity and market attractiveness signals.
- **Weed Strategy Designer:** District-specific weed, timing, product, application, expected impact, and a proposed plan for villages, retailers, demo plots, meetings, digital activity, and timeline.
- **Crop Lifecycle Tracking:** Acreage split across crop growth stages, with the herbicide window indicated.
- **Execution Monitor and Heat Map:** Target-versus-achieved counts/percentages for meetings, demos, retail calls, village coverage, and estimated spray adoption.
- **Competitor Threat Monitor:** District, major competitor, presence, activity, and trend.
- **Sales & Liquidation Outcome:** Primary/secondary sales, liquidation, covered acreage, farmer reach, and ROI.
- **AI Strategic Recommendations & Alerts** and a time-phased **Suggested Action Roadmap**.

The matrix and heat map answer different questions: the matrix recommends **where and how to compete**, while the execution heat map shows **whether planned activities are being completed**.

### C. Segmentation Tool

This workspace contains four numbered views.

#### 1. Opportunity Map — the linked/default view

This is the central opportunity-planning page for the selected crop, state, season, and product.

- **Top-line KPIs:** Addressable market, three tier potentials, actionable acreage and mandals, average dissatisfaction, cost saving, and an Ashitaka share ambition.
- **Market Classification Map:** A mandal-level map colored by action tier: **Easy to Win**, **Competitive**, **No Go**, and **No Data**. The legend shows opportunity value and mandal count for the first three tiers.
- **Tier Potential Summary:** Each tier is described using weed pressure, awareness, product fit, average influence, and a representative mandal. The displayed actions are Scale & Capture, Selective Convert, and Monitor Only.
- **Segment-Wise Competitor Intelligence:** The dominant brands, farmer satisfaction, and vulnerability are summarized separately for each tier.
- **Action Playbook:** Example tactics are grouped as Direct Attack, Tank-Mix, Premix, and Monitor.
- **Actionable Opportunity Engine:** A ranked mandal table with action, acreage, value, and pack.
- **Selected Mandal Detail:** A selected geography’s recoverable opportunity, acreage, conversion score, dissatisfaction, leading competitor/share, satisfaction, dominant weed, recommended pack, and timing.

The most useful interpretation is: **the tier map ranks local commercial actionability, not just weed severity**. A “No Go” label means the model is recommending low investment under its assumptions; it does not mean there is no agricultural need.

#### 2. Weed Flora Hotspot

This page can be switched among five weed groups, including Crabgrass (*Digitaria* spp.), Crowfoot Grass, Barnyard Grass, Foxtail, and Congress/Pigweed groups.

- **Hotspot KPI cards:** Priority district count, top hotspot, fastest-growing district, average trend, data confidence, severity index, expansion risk, and dominant control mix.
- **AI Hotspot Insights:** Narrative about severity, growth, spread, and control dependence.
- **Weed Hotspot Map:** District-level severity view with Low, Watch, High, Critical, and No Data categories.
- **District Trend Leaderboard:** Rank, district, trend change, and score.
- **Hotspot Trend Analytics:** State prevalence over months, district severity mix, and spread pattern.
- **Control Mix & Market Players:** Current control approaches, alternate products, and estimated market influence.
- **Priority District Action Engine:** Severity, trend, control signal, and recommended surveillance/rotation/retailer actions.
- **Selected Weed Detail:** Weed identity and class plus hotspot, growth, confidence, spread, control-pressure, and outlook signals.

This is the **weed-specific surveillance view**. It is useful for detecting where a particular weed is severe or expanding; it should be read alongside the crop, season, and field-level evidence.

#### 3. Competition Battle Map

This is a competitor-pressure and conversion view.

- **Summary KPIs:** Red Ocean, Competitive, and Open Opportunity market counts; vulnerable mandals; tank-mix mandals; recoverable opportunity; a conversion-trigger score; and priority geography counts.
- **Competition Intensity Map:** District/market categories based on competitor control, divided into Open Opportunity, Competitive, Red Ocean, and No Data.
- **Vulnerability / Conversion Quadrant:** District bubbles positioned by competitor strength and conversion potential; bubble size represents recoverable rupees. The four strategic zones are Direct Attack, Selective Activation, Tank-Mix Play, and Monitor/Defend.
- **Selected Battle Zone:** A selected geography’s competitor strength, conversion potential, mandal count, recoverable opportunity, vulnerability, leading competitors, reason to switch, and weed spectrum.
- **Competition Summary:** Characteristics and suggested treatment for Red Ocean, Competitive, and Open Opportunity markets.
- **Conversion Triggers & Competitor Pressure:** Trigger counts such as weed dissatisfaction, price sensitivity, and tank-mix suitability, alongside competitor-strength scores.
- **Priority District/Mandal Engine:** Ranked localities with action, vulnerability, recoverable value, and a key trigger.

This map answers **where competitor pressure is strong and where a switch may be plausible**. “Open opportunity” is a model classification; it should not be equated automatically with proven demand.

#### 4. Micro Market Intelligence

This is the most granular segmentation page inspected. It summarizes tier counts/value/acreage, then provides a directory of 302 mandals for the selected view.

The directory can be searched and filtered by district and tier. Its rows include mandal, district, crop, main weed, competitor product, vulnerability score, target area in acres, opportunity in rupees, and an action/view control.

Use this page to turn a state or district priority into a **specific mandal shortlist** for field planning. Verify the row-level figures and action recommendation before using them in a target or budget.

### D. Product Launch Roadmap

The inspected roadmap is framed around Cyperus rotundus (sedge) control in Maharashtra maize and offers seven views:

1. Executive Summary
2. Whitespace Map
3. Need-State Analysis
4. Competitor Gap Analyzer
5. Product Concept Recommendation
6. Launch Wave Roadmap
7. Readiness & Action Center

The visible executive summary includes affected acreage, untapped opportunity, potential revenue, whitespace score, GTM readiness, priority-entry districts, and a recommended concept. Supporting sections include:

- **Strategic Launch Whitespace Map:** District whitespace intensity and tier, with highlighted clusters.
- **AI Executive Narrative:** Summarizes unmet need, severity/control gap, and the recommended concept.
- **Launch Opportunity Scorecard:** Composite whitespace score plus prevalence gap, control gap, competitive potential, and readiness components.
- **Top Unmet-Need Districts:** Ranks locations using infestation, control gap, vulnerability, whitespace, and tier.
- **Recommended Product Concept:** A selective post-emergence sedge-control concept, with proposed product, pack, and price logic.
- **Launch Wave Snapshot:** Phased groups of districts/mandals with opportunity value and rationale.
- **Approval/launch checklist:** Concept validation, dossier, district selection, and pilot readiness.

This is a **product-portfolio hypothesis and launch-planning workflow**, not evidence that a concept is registered, safe, efficacious, commercially approved, or ready to sell. All active ingredient, formulation, crop-use, dose, timing, pack, price, and claims shown in the UI require independent regulatory, agronomic, supply, and finance validation.

### E. Reports

Reports provides a reporting workflow:

1. Choose a scope: Region, Territory, District, or Mandal.
2. Choose state/crop/season/date range and an executive report or raw-data export.
3. Choose a template: Executive Strategy, Competitive, Spray Window, Pack & Price, or Launch Readiness.
4. Configure commentary, appendix, charts, and raw tables.
5. Preview a paginated report.
6. Generate/export PDF, PPT, or Excel, then share with organization users or external email and optionally schedule delivery.
7. Browse recent reports and saved templates.

These are capabilities presented by the interface. The inspection confirms the controls and previews exist, but does not independently verify every generation, export, delivery, or scheduling action.

## 4. What the inspected example is saying

For the default **Kharif 2024 / Maharashtra / Maize / Ashitaka** scenario, the application’s narrative is broadly:

- It represents a survey footprint of about **19,222 villages** and **8.31 lakh acres** in Market Intelligence.
- It highlights districts such as **Nanded, Latur, and Parbhani** for high weed pressure and field attention.
- It treats the **12–20 DAS** period as a key intervention window in several views.
- It proposes prioritizing mandals by a combination of weed pressure, product fit, competition, dissatisfaction, and field/channel conditions.
- It presents **Tier 1 / Easy to Win** markets for more aggressive scaling, Tier 2 / Competitive markets for selective conversion, and Tier 3 / No Go markets for monitoring or lower investment.
- It also suggests that competitor dissatisfaction, price sensitivity, product fit, availability, retailer engagement, demonstrations, and coverage can be levers for conversion.

This is an interpretation of the platform’s own displayed scenario—not a claim that those numbers or recommendations are independently verified.

## 6. Important interpretation and data-quality cautions

Before using this platform for operational, financial, or agronomic decisions, confirm the following:

1. **Data provenance and freshness:** The site has a “Live” label, while the selected season is Kharif 2024 and some timelines mention 2025. No clear source, refresh timestamp, survey methodology, or current-season status was visible.
2. **Scenario numbers vary across pages:** Segmentation opportunity tiers show ₹12.8 Cr + ₹5.1 Cr + ₹2.1 Cr = ₹20 Cr. Other views show an ₹80.6 Cr market/sales-related figure and a TPM/TAM/SAM/SOM sequence of ₹80.6/₹60.5/₹34.7/₹24.2 Cr. These may represent different scopes or definitions, but the UI does not reconcile them.
3. **Other value mismatches need explanation:** Product Launch Roadmap shows ₹9.6 Cr potential launch revenue while the Strategy Command Center shows ₹5.9 Cr launch opportunity. A Strategy Command Center pack preview displays ₹420 Cr revenue uplift, much larger than other nearby figures. Verify units, calculation logic, and whether the latter is a display/data error.
4. **Metric definitions are not exposed:** The exact formulas for infestation, opportunity, vulnerability, conversion, whitespace, confidence, influence, and “AI” recommendations are not provided in the inspected interface.
5. **Denominators and meanings may differ:** Acreage, village coverage, market share, farmer satisfaction, and demand values should be checked for their populations, time periods, and units. “Share” may refer to a brand, company, product, or an ambition; labels do not always make the distinction explicit.
6. **AI narratives need validation:** Treat generated insights as prompts for investigation, not as evidence or autonomous recommendations. Confirm them against field observations, reliable source data, and responsible agronomic/regulatory review.
7. **Agronomy and regulation:** Product names, application windows, active ingredients, mixes, packs, prices, and crop claims shown in the interface are not a substitute for current product labels, local registrations, expert advice, or safety guidance.
8. **Recent reports and “Live” status are UI labels:** The interface alone does not establish that the displayed figures are current, that reports were actually generated recently, or that exports/sharing work end-to-end.

## 7. A practical way to explore the platform

1. Set and record the correct season, geography, crop, and product.
2. Start in **Market Intelligence → Executive Summary** to understand survey coverage, crop/weed context, and broad market conditions.
3. Use **District Intelligence** to inspect the priority district and verify its local weed mix, competitors, coverage, and timing.
4. Open **Segmentation → Opportunity Map** to understand the tier and recommended action.
5. Use **Competition Battle Map** to compare conversion potential against competitor pressure.
6. Use **Micro Market Intelligence** to find the specific mandals and inspect their row-level values.
7. Use **Strategy & Execution** to connect priorities to planned activities and track achievement.
8. Review **Product Launch Roadmap** only when evaluating a new product concept; validate regulatory, efficacy, market, and financial assumptions separately.
9. Generate a report only after confirming the scope, timeframe, definitions, and selected data.

## 8. Bottom line

WeedIntel is best understood as a **geospatial herbicide market-planning and execution dashboard**. Its strongest conceptual value is the flow from weed/market signals to district and mandal priorities, recommended field plays, execution tracking, and reporting. Its maps are layered decision aids: some describe agricultural pressure, others commercial opportunity, competitive intensity, launch whitespace, or execution progress.

The platform is useful for understanding how such a GTM workflow could be organized. The displayed figures and recommendations, however, need validation—especially because the inspected interface mixes historical seasons, future planning references, and financial figures that are not clearly reconciled.
